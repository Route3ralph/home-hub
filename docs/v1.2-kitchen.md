# Home Hub v1.2 — Food: dinners and the shopping list (design)

This is the build contract for v1.2: one shared shopping list and a dinner plan. It is written against the hub as it is live: schema version 3, that is `0002_home_hub_v2.sql` and `0003_google_calendar.sql`, the `hub-jobs` Edge Function and `supabase/cron.sql`. Line references are to the repository at `ef19532` (`7df4bb8` changes only the README). Every table, column and function named here either exists there or is added by migration `0004_meals_and_shopping.sql` (§3). It was put together from three independent drafts (a minimal one, a helper-first one and an integration-first one); §17 says what came from where and what was left out.

What the owner asked for: "i also want to add a shopping list, meal planning etc to this". The family wants it plain and low-upkeep. Four rules follow:
- **One list, one plan.** Home Hub's list and dinners are the family's. Hearth's own Meals tab and Groceries list stay unused (§11).
- **Nothing to keep up.** No recipe box, no pantry, no staples schedule, no lists to manage. An empty day or an empty list is normal.
- **Taylor can use it from the link she already has,** in Spanish, with the tick and Undo she already knows from tasks.
- **Same output when unused.** With no meals and no list, the email, the texts, My day and every event on the wall come out byte for byte as on schema 3 (§3.6).

Examples use the realdb household (`tests/realdb/seed_v2.sql`), as v1.1 does:
- Alex and Morgan are the parents (admins).
- Sam is an adult who signs in.
- Jordan is a kid.
- Taylor is the helper, and reads Spanish.

The database tests' household (`tests/db/fixtures.sql`) differs: there Alex is the admin, Sam the adult, Jordan a parent who was removed, Taylor the kid and Morgan the Spanish-speaking helper. §13 names people as each suite has them.

The page is called **Food**. It is never called "Kitchen": the renovation project on the Projects page is already "Kitchen" ("Kitchen: Tile starts").

## 0. Decisions on one page

- **One PR, one migration, schema version 4.** The Food tab and every new request appear only on version 4, so the site goes live first and the migration after (§15), as CLAUDE.md requires. No Edge Function change and no `cron.sql` change, so nothing to redeploy or re-run.
- **A new page, Food** (`#/food`): this week's dinners in one row at the top, the shopping list below. It gets a button in the phone's bottom bar: Today · Calendar · ＋ · Tasks · Food · More.
- **One shopping list.** One row per thing the household buys (and per lock), kept after it is bought. That row is the item's history, the suggestions as you type, and the guard against duplicates.
  - Typing something already there brings that line back instead of adding a second one: "Milk", "milk " and "2 milk" are one item, and so is "Leche" while "Milk" is to buy (a built-in English and Spanish word list).
  - "2 milk", "2 lb ground beef" and "Milk: 2 gallons" put the amount in a small note, so the name stays the same.
  - **Tap = bought.** The row is struck through where it is, then moves to "Just bought", with a "Bought · Undo" toast. **Buy again** (folded away) lists everything bought in the last 60 days, so the weekly milk is one tap.
  - **Aisle groups, automatic.** Ten fixed groups in store order, cold things last, guessed from the same word list. A correction sticks, because it is saved on the item's own row.
  - **A lock for private things** (pharmacy): a locked line never reaches a My day link, and nothing typed on a link can find or change it. Every grown-up who signs in still sees it.
- **Dinner, one line a day.** A name ("Tacos") or one tap: Leftovers · Eating out · Takeout · Fend for yourself. Optional: who cooks (who's on dinner, for leftovers and takeout), a note for them, a recipe link, and a lock that keeps the title off the wall, My day links and email subjects. Empty days are fine. The table has a `slot` column, so lunch later is a page change, not a migration.
- **Prep reminders are ordinary tasks.** "Add a prep reminder…" on a saved dinner opens the task form, filled in for the day before (never before today) and the cook; a locked dinner's is a private task.
- **Dinner shows** on Today, on every My day, at the end of Today in the morning email, in the cook's "Send their day" text, and on the wall as one all-day "Dinner: Tacos (Sam)" on **Hub – Family** once the v1.1 calendars are connected. Past dinners stay on Google.
- **Taylor's My day** gets a "Lista de compras · 7" button that opens the list: Spanish aisle headings, big rows, tick, Undo, add. Limits, counted per call: helpers only, 30 changes to the list and 100 ticks a day, adds from My day stop at 150 things to buy, locked lines never shown or touched. Parents choose Off · See and tick · See, tick and add (default: all three).
- **Two shoppers, bad signal.** Every write names the state it was made on and carries its own key, so a resend changes nothing (the database does a change once per key) and a change that waited offline never undoes a newer one. The list checks for changes every 10 seconds while it is on screen, and no row moves under a finger. Unsent changes wait on the phone with a "Not sent yet" mark. No Supabase Realtime and no service worker.
- **＋ adds a shopping item too.** On version 4 the Add sheet offers Event · Task · Shopping.
- **The migration wraps, it doesn't copy.** `digest_for` (280 lines) and `private.calendar_desired` (250 lines) keep their 0003 bodies byte for byte under a `_v3` name. Thin new versions add the meal rows on top. Smaller functions (`get_person_day`, `day_text`, `calendar_plan`) are replaced with their changes marked.
- **Owner's time:** about 5 minutes to paste the migration, then a 10-minute try-out.

## 1. Decisions for the owner

Each has a default. The build goes ahead on the defaults, and every one of them can be changed later. Only 1 is hard to undo once the family is used to it.

1. **Use Home Hub's list and dinners, not Hearth's own Meals tab and Groceries list.** Default: **Home Hub.** Why: nothing outside Hearth can read or fill Hearth's Meals or Groceries. Home Hub's reach every phone, Taylor's My day in Spanish, the morning email and the wall. Using both shows every dinner twice on the wall, and two grocery lists disagree. What it costs: anything someone adds in Hearth's Meals or Groceries reaches neither Taylor, the email nor the hub, and our list isn't on the wall (it can be added later as one all-day line). At the v1.1 Hearth sitting we hide Hearth's Meals and Groceries if Hearth allows it.
2. **Taylor and the list on her My day: see it, tick things off, add things.** Default: **all three.** Why: she does much of the shopping and notices what has run out, and she needs no account. A parent can change it on the Food page: Off · See and tick · See, tick and add.
3. **Anyone who has Taylor's link can see the list** (her link may sit in a WhatsApp chat). Default: **one list, with a lock on private items** (pharmacy). A locked line never reaches her link, and nothing typed on her link can find or change it; every grown-up who signs in (Alex, Morgan, Sam) still sees it, so it is not for gifts. Why: one list is simpler than two, and the lock covers the private case. A grown-ups-only or parents-only list is in Later.
4. **Jordan (kid) sees dinners on his My day, but not the list.** Default: **dinner yes, list no.** Why: kids like knowing what's for dinner; the list isn't theirs.
5. **Dinner only, or lunch and breakfast too?** Default: **dinner only.** Why: dinner is what families plan, and extra slots are what make other apps feel heavy. Lunch later needs no database change.
6. **Who cooks: optional on each dinner** (for Leftovers and Takeout: who's on dinner). Default: **optional.** That person sees the note and the recipe link on their My day and in their email, and gets an email on a day that would otherwise send them none. Why: it tells Taylor or Sam they're cooking, or heating up the lasagna, without a separate task.
7. **Dinner on the kitchen wall:** "Dinner: Tacos (Sam)" as an all-day item in the Family column, and past dinners stay. Default: **on, all day** (a switch on the Food page; a locked dinner shows only as "Dinner: Private item"). Why: the wall is where everyone looks, and all-day doesn't block the day. Look at it once the calendars are connected; a set time (6pm) is the alternative.
8. **Dinner in the morning email:** one line at the end of Today for everyone who gets an email. On a day with nothing else, the subject says "Dinner: Tacos" instead of "Nothing planned" ("Dinner: Private item" when the dinner is locked). Default: **yes.** Dinner alone sends an email only to the cook (or whoever's on dinner).
9. **Who can change dinners and the list:** every grown-up who signs in (Alex, Morgan, Sam), as for tasks. The two switches (Taylor's list, dinners on the wall) are for parents. Default: **as described.**
10. **Where Food goes on a phone:** its own button in the bottom bar (6 buttons). Default: **bottom bar.** Why: the list is used one-handed in a store. If six buttons feel crowded, it moves under More.

## 3. Database: migration `0004_meals_and_shopping.sql`

A fresh install is `schema.sql`, then `0002`, `0003` and `0004`. Like the others, it runs as one transaction and is additive, and every rule in v2-design §3 applies:
- RLS on every table.
- `set search_path = ''` and schema-qualified names in every function.
- `revoke execute … from public, anon` on every new function, public and private, then a grant to whoever calls it. (0002's blanket revoke on `private` ran once.)
- Audit columns come from `private.stamp()`. Every time the functions write is `private.now()`.
- **The migration seeds no rows** in the new tables in `public`. The same-output check depends on it. (`private.food_pairs`, `food_other`'s half of the word list, is filled once from `private.food_words()`, §3.4.)

### 3.1 Guard and version

```sql
begin;
do $$
begin
  if to_regprocedure('public.hub_schema_version()') is null then
    raise exception 'Home Hub v2 (migration 0002) isn''t installed. Run 0002_home_hub_v2.sql and 0003_google_calendar.sql first. Nothing was changed.';
  end if;
  if public.hub_schema_version() < 3 then
    raise exception 'Google Calendar (migration 0003) isn''t installed (hub_schema_version() is %). Run 0003_google_calendar.sql first. Nothing was changed.',
                    public.hub_schema_version();
  end if;
  if public.hub_schema_version() >= 4 then
    raise exception 'Meals and shopping (migration 0004) is already installed (hub_schema_version() is %). Nothing was changed.',
                    public.hub_schema_version();
  end if;
  if to_regclass('public.meals') is not null then
    raise exception 'Meals and shopping (migration 0004) is already installed, but hub_schema_version() was set back to 3. To switch it on again, make hub_schema_version() return 4 (docs/v1.2-kitchen.md §15). Nothing was changed.';
  end if;
  -- ... and the shape check below.
end;
$$;
```

The set-back check comes before the shape check, so a database rolled back as §15 says gets the right message rather than "isn't as 0003 left it". The shape check, in the same block: `public.digest_for` and `private.calendar_desired` exist with 0003's argument types; neither `private.digest_for_v3` nor `private.calendar_desired_v3` exists; and `pg_constraint` has `calendar_links_item_kind_check` on `public.calendar_links`. Otherwise it raises "This database isn't as migration 0003 left it. Nothing was changed."

The migration ends with `create or replace function public.hub_schema_version()` returning 4 (`create or replace` keeps the grants), then `commit;`. 0003's own guard (`<> 2`) still says "already installed" on a version-4 database, so `tests/db/run.sh:55-57` keeps passing.

Notes:
- Without the wider kind check, `calendar_record` couldn't store a meal's link, and `calendar_store_events` would silently skip adopting one (its adoption catches every error, 0003:1151-1160).
- A stored generated column is computed after BEFORE triggers, so `name_key` is the cleaned name, and `on conflict (name_key, private)` finds the unique index (a plain one, not partial, so `on conflict` needs no index predicate).
- The household columns follow `household`'s policies: members read, admins update.
- `food_pairs` is a table so that `food_other` is a lookup: worked out on every call, the ~300 normalized pairs cost about 3.5 ms, and `food_other` runs once per line in every poll of the list (about 1 s a poll at the 200-line cap, close to the 3-second statement timeout Supabase gives anon). As a lookup it is under 0.1 ms. `food.test.sql` holds it to the old per-call version (kept in the test) for every word in both languages, and times both polls at the cap; `access.test.sql` checks that every table in `private` has RLS, no policies and no privilege for anon, authenticated or service_role.
- `my_day_use` is a table rather than a count over `shopping_items`, because a count of rows in their current state misses calls that change a row without changing who added or bought it (a note appended, a tick undone), and two parallel calls would each see the old count (§4.1).

**Table grants.** Supabase's default privileges would give anon and authenticated everything on a new table, truncate included. As 0003 does (0003:393-402):
```
revoke all on public.meals, public.shopping_items, public.my_day_use from public, anon, authenticated;
grant select, delete on public.meals to authenticated;
grant insert (day, slot, kind, title, cook_id, note, link, private), update (day, slot, kind, title, cook_id, note, link, private)
  on public.meals to authenticated;
grant select, delete on public.shopping_items to authenticated;
grant update (name, note, aisle, private) on public.shopping_items to authenticated;
revoke all on private.food_pairs from public, anon, authenticated, service_role;
revoke all on private.list_done from public, anon, authenticated, service_role;
```
`service_role` keeps Supabase's defaults: `digest_for` and the calendar job read `meals` and `shopping_items` as it.

### 3.4 Functions

**Pages** (STABLE, SECURITY INVOKER; revoke from public, anon; grant to authenticated, service_role):
- `shopping_list() returns jsonb`. Row-level security decides, so a non-member gets empty lists.
  ```
  { as_of: '2026-10-27T15:42',                                           -- the household's local date and time
    to_buy: [{ id, name, note, aisle, private, added_by, seen, other }],   -- A–Z by name_key, then id
    bought: [{ id, name, note, aisle, private, bought_by, seen }] }        -- everything bought since the household's
                                                                           -- midnight, and the caller's own ticks of the
                                                                           -- last 15 minutes (as My day's list keeps hers):
                                                                           -- bought_at desc, name_key, id
  ```
  - `added_by` and `bought_by` are person ids; the page draws them with the core's `personDot`.
  - `seen` is the row's state as the phone saw it: `added_at` for a line to buy, `bought_at` for a bought one, as `to_jsonb` writes a `timestamptz` (microseconds kept). The page keeps it as that string, never as a `Date`, and sends it back as `p_seen` (§4.2).
  - `other` is the same thing in the reader's language when the name is a known word in the other one (`private.food_other`, the reader's `people.language` through `private.shop_who()`): "Leche" carries `other: 'milk'` for Alex. Left out when there is none.
  - The snapshot stays small for the 10-second poll: what is to buy, and today's buys. **Buy again** (§6.1) comes from the page's own table read, not from here.

**Signed-in actions** (SECURITY DEFINER, in `public` because the page calls them; revoke from public, anon, service_role; grant to authenticated). Each raises 42501 "Only the family can change the shopping list." unless `private.is_member()`, as `complete_task` does:
- `add_to_list(p_name text, p_private boolean default false, p_key uuid default null) returns jsonb` → `private.list_add_once(p_key, p_name, p_private, (private.shop_who()).id, false)`, then the snapshot: `{result: 'added' | 'back' | 'already', item_id, name} || shopping_list()`. `name` is the line's own name ("Milk" when "Leche" joined it).
- `buy_item(p_item uuid, p_bought boolean, p_seen timestamptz, p_key uuid default null) returns jsonb` → `{changed: <private.list_buy_once(p_key, …)>} || shopping_list()`.

## 4. The shopping list's rules

### 4.1 Adding (`private.list_add`)

1. **Clean and split.** `s := private.clean_text(left(p_name, 200), 100)`: cut first, so a huge input costs nothing (My day has already refused anything over 200 characters). Then a leading amount, matched case-insensitively:
   ```
   ^([0-9]{1,3})(?:\s*x)?\s+(?:(lbs?|oz|kg|g|dozen|packs?|bags?|cans?|bottles?|box(?:es)?|jars?|gallons?|bunch(?:es)?|libras?|kilos?|docenas?|paquetes?|bolsas?|latas?|botellas?|cajas?)\s+(?:(?:of|de)\s+)?)?([[:alpha:]].{2,})$
   ```
   That is a number of 1 to 3 digits, an optional "x", a space, an optional unit word (and "of" or "de" after it), then a name of at least 3 characters that starts with a letter. The number and the unit become the note:

   | Typed | Name | Note |
   |---|---|---|
   | "2 milk", "2x milk", "2 x milk" | milk | 2 |
   | "12 huevos" | huevos | 12 |
   | "2 lb ground beef" | ground beef | 2 lb |
   | "1 dozen eggs" | eggs | 1 dozen |
   | "3 cans of black beans" | black beans | 3 cans |
   | "2 bolsas de arroz" | arroz | 2 bolsas |
   | "2% milk", "7up", "7 Up" | as typed | none |

   Otherwise the text after the first ":" is the note: "Milk: 2 gallons" → name "Milk", note "2 gallons". Then the name is cleaned to at most 60 characters and the note to at most 80. An empty name → 22023 "Type what to buy."
2. **Which line.** A locked and an unlocked line are separate (§3.2), and the lock decides where to look:
   - **From My day:** only the unlocked line. A locked line is never looked at, so her "pregnancy test" becomes its own visible line and the locked one is untouched.
   - **Signed in, lock off:** the unlocked line; if there is none, the locked one (a re-add never unlocks: grown-ups see the lock).
   - **Signed in, lock on:** the locked line; if there is none, the unlocked one, which becomes locked (in step 6 or 7).
   - **Other language.** If no line has the name, and the name is a known word (`private.food_other`), the twin's line counts as found, but only while it is to buy and only where the rules above may look. "Leche" while "Milk" is to buy → that line, `'already'`. A bought twin isn't brought back: the new name gets its own line.
   - The line found is locked (`for update`) before anything else.
3. **Nothing to change.** The line is to buy, its lock stays as it is, and the call brings no note, or one already in its note (the whole note, or a run of its " · " parts, so "2 · los grandes" typed or sent again finds "2 · los grandes"; "2" alone is not in "12"): `'already'`, and nothing is counted. So a resend after a lost reply is never refused, even at the cap (a resend with its key isn't even looked at: it gets the first answer, §8).
4. **Counted, from My day.** Anything else first calls `private.count_my_day(p_by, 'changes')`: past 30 a day → P0001 "Too many changes from My day today."
5. **None found.** If 200 things are already to buy (150 from My day, so the family can always add) → 54000 "The list is full." Otherwise insert it with `aisle = private.guess_aisle(<the parsed name>)`, `private = p_private` (always false from My day), `added_at = private.now()`, `added_by = p_by`, using `on conflict (name_key, private) do nothing returning id`. If nothing was inserted (another phone added the same name a moment before), select that line again `for update` and go on with step 6 or 7, so this call's note isn't lost. Result `'added'`.
6. **To buy already:** it stays one line. The new note is added after " · " (at most 80 characters), unless it is in the note already (step 3). Result `'already'`.
7. **Bought:** it goes back on the list (the 200 / 150 check first): `bought_at`, `bought_by` null; the note is the new one (or none); `added_at`, `added_by` now. Its aisle stays, so a correction sticks. Result `'back'`.
8. From My day, `updated_by` is "<name> (My day)". Signed in, `private.stamp()` sets the email. The reply's `name` is the line's own name, so the toast can say "Already on the list as Milk".

### 4.2 Ticking (`private.list_buy`)

- `p_bought` null → 22023 "Choose bought or not."; `p_seen` null → 22023 "Reload the list to see it as it is now."
- The row is selected `for update`. From My day a locked row counts as no row. No row → `false`.
- **Bought:** changes only a row with `bought_at is null and added_at = p_seen`: `bought_at = private.now(), bought_by = p_by`. Anything else → `false`: someone else bought it first, or it was bought and put back on the list since this phone looked, so a tick that waited offline never buys the new need.
- **Not bought** (Undo, or putting something back): changes only a row with `bought_at = p_seen`, so a put-back that waited offline never reopens someone else's newer purchase. Anything else → `false`.
  - From My day, also: only her own tick (`bought_by` = her) of the last 15 minutes. A matching row that isn't → 42501 "Only your own tick can be undone, for 15 minutes." (She can add it again instead.)
  - Signed in: any bought row.
  - Bought less than 15 minutes ago, it's an Undo: `added_by` stays. Older, it's going back on the list: `added_by = p_by`. Either way `added_at = private.now()`. `added_at` is the line's `seen`, so the line is never back in the state a tick was sent against: a tick whose reply was lost, sent again after someone's Undo, gets `false` instead of buying the line again (and quietly cancelling the Undo).
- **Counted, from My day:** a tick or Undo that is about to change a row first calls `private.count_my_day(p_by, 'ticks')`: past 100 a day → P0001 "Too many ticks from My day today." Calls that change nothing aren't counted.
- Every write is a "set" against the state the phone saw, never a toggle, and carries its own key (§8). A resend after a lost reply gets the first try's answer (`true` when it was done) and changes nothing; without a key it finds the row already changed and gets `false`, and the page sees the item is already as it wanted and says nothing. A change that waited while someone else changed the row gets `false`; the page shows the list as it is now, with "Changed on another phone".

### 4.3 Editing and forgetting (signed in only)

- The page updates `name`, `note`, `aisle` and `private` directly (column grants). The aisle is a row of one-tap chips at the top of Edit.
- A rename or a lock change that would make two lines with the same name and the same lock fails with 23505. The page says "There's already a line called Milk." and offers **Merge**: Forget this line, then `add_to_list(<the other line's name>, …)` with this line's note and lock, so the note joins and a bought line comes back on the list.
- **Forget** asks first ("Forget Milk? It won't be suggested again.") and deletes the row, for a typo that shouldn't come back in suggestions.
- Nothing else is ever deleted: the household buys a few hundred different things, so the rows stay.

## 5. Dinners

- **One row per (day, slot).** v1.2's pages write `slot = 'dinner'` only. Everything in SQL handles any slot (its word from `private.food_word`; ordered breakfast, lunch, dinner with `array_position(array['breakfast', 'lunch', 'dinner'], slot)`, never `order by slot`, which is alphabetical), so lunch later is a page change.
- **Kinds.** `cook` needs a title ("Tacos"). The quick kinds have none and show their word: Leftovers / Sobras, Eating out / Comemos fuera, Takeout / Comida para llevar, Fend for yourself / Cada uno se arregla.
- **Who's on it, note, link** (all optional). A cooked meal has a cook; Leftovers and Takeout have "who's on dinner" (Taylor heats up the lasagna); Eating out and Fend for yourself have nobody. The link is for a cooked meal only. The note and link show to grown-ups on the Food page and Today, and to the person on it on their My day and in their email. - **Locked** (`private`, off unless set; "Keep the title private" under More). ## 8. Two shoppers, bad signal

**Freshness, not Realtime.** While the list is on screen and the page is visible, the Food page calls `shopping_list()` and My day calls `get_list_by_token` every 10 seconds, and at once on focus, `visibilitychange` and `online`. One request at a time, none while a write is being sent, and no redraw when the answer is the same (both compared without `as_of`), so the add box, its focus and the scroll never jump. A change waits until 3 seconds after the last tap on the list, so no row moves under a finger (§6.1). The timers stop on `ctx.signal` (Food) or when the view closes (My day). Two phones on the list: 12 small requests a minute; none when closed. The snapshot holds only what is to buy and today's buys, so each answer stays small.

Why not Supabase Realtime: `postgres_changes` follows row-level security, so the anonymous My day page would get nothing; the migration would need a publication that `tests/db/standins.sql` doesn't have; and neither `mock.js` nor `realdb/server.js` speaks websockets, so it couldn't be tested.

**The outbox** (`shoplist.js`, used the same way by `food.js` and `day.js`):
1. **Every tap shows at once** and is queued: `{op: 'add' | 'buy' | 'unbuy', key, item?, seen?, name?, private?, at}`. `key` is the change's own key, a random uuid (version 4, `crypto.randomUUID`), sent as `p_key` with every try of it. A buy or unbuy carries the `seen` of the row as the phone showed it. An unbuy queued behind its own buy (Undo pressed before the buy's reply) takes `seen` from that buy's reply. A buy of a line that a waiting add brings back on the list (its name typed again, or My day's "¿Añadir otra vez?" and Comprar otra vez) waits for that add and takes `seen` from its reply, since the add gives the line a new `added_at`. The page draws a waiting add on the line the database will take (§4.1: the one with this lock first, to buy or bought, then the other lock's), but the list has only today's buys: when the add lands on another line (one with this lock bought before today, or the drawn one was forgotten), the buy waiting for it, and anything waiting behind that buy, goes to the line it landed on. A row that a waiting change moved has no `seen` of its own until that change is answered, so a tap on it takes `seen` from that change's reply, or from the list in it, never the state from before the change. The same goes for a row tapped again where it is, before it moves (ticked and unticked, or the toast's Undo, which unticks the row wherever it is drawn then: still under To buy, or already moved to Just bought / Comprado hoy while a tap holds the list): the page sends it by id, so its `seen` comes from the last change's reply or the list now, never the one it was drawn with, which an un-tick has replaced (§4.2). An Undo given only the item (from a tick's toast, or a row ticked a moment ago) undoes only this phone's own purchase (on Food, bought by the signed-in person; for a member with no person, bought with no `bought_by`, as the database records that member's ticks), or the one this phone was asked to put back by a tap on the row as drawn (a put-back that a tick then cancelled before it left the phone, so the next un-tick goes with the same `seen`): when the tick found the line already bought by someone else, nothing is sent and the page says "Changed on another phone". It goes with the `seen` of the tick's reply (§6.1), which the outbox keeps while the page is open, so it still works after a read past the household's midnight and the tick's 15 minutes has taken the line out of Just bought (`bought` is since midnight, plus the caller's own ticks of the last 15 minutes, §3.4); a tick whose reply was lost and that is sent again just after midnight finds the line in that answer's `bought` (the caller's own tick), so the Undo waiting behind it still goes with its `seen`; if the line was bought again since by someone else, the answer is `false` and the page says "Changed on another phone".
2. **The queue and the last snapshot are saved** in `localStorage`, and only for whoever made them:
   - the Food page: `homehub.shop.hub`, holding the signed-in person's id (from the core's `mePerson`). Anything saved for another person is dropped unread, and `signOut` removes the key (§6.3);
   - My day: `homehub.shop.day`, holding the first 16 hex characters of the token's SHA-256 (`crypto.subtle`), never the token. Anything saved for another link is dropped unread. When any token function answers null (an unknown or replaced link, a person no longer active, or the switch Off), the key is removed.
   - Every read and write is in `try/catch`; the page works without it.
   - A change still on its way when the page closes (leaving Food) stays saved. If its answer comes anyway, that change comes off the saved queue by its id, never by saving the closed page's queue over a newer page's, the changes waiting for it get their `seen` from the answer, and the list in the answer becomes the saved list (unless a newer one is saved there, by its `as_of`), so the next visit shows that change done even before a read, or without one. A page opened again in the same window that still has it takes that answer as soon as it comes, even before it sends its own saved queue, so a tap there never saves the answered change back; one already sending it waits for that answer instead of sending it again. With no answer it goes on the next visit.
3. **Sent one at a time, in order.** A buy and an unbuy of the same item cancel out only while the first of them has never left the phone and no add waits after it: an add reads the line as the changes before it leave it (an add of a line an unbuy puts back joins it; without the unbuy it would bring the bought line back, and the tap would be lost). Otherwise both are sent, in order; with `seen` from the first one's reply the second matches.
4. **What an answer means:**
   - **Success:** drop it, take the snapshot in the reply, and lay what is still waiting over it. `changed: false` means the row was no longer as this phone saw it: if the server's row is already as the tap wanted (someone else did the same, or a resend without a key after a lost reply), say nothing; otherwise show the server's list with "Changed on another phone". A change queued behind it goes on, with the row's `seen`, only when the change was this phone's own: it changed the row (a resend's answer is the first try's, so one whose answer was lost says `changed: true`), or it is a buy that finds the line bought by this phone. A buy that finds the line bought by someone else never is, so the Undo behind it is dropped and their purchase stays. Nor is an unbuy answered `changed: false`: one that never got through, done only now after someone else put the line back, finds it to buy with their new `seen`, so the buy behind it is dropped ("Changed on another phone") and never buys their new need.
   - **Null from a token function:** the link no longer works. Clear the queue and the saved list, and show `day.js`'s existing `bad_link` screen.
   - **A refusal:** an answer carrying a database error code (42501, 22023, P0001, 54000, 23505) or a PostgREST `PGRST…` code. Drop that change, show the server's list and one toast (`friendly()`).
   - **No answer, or a 5xx, 408 or 429:** keep everything, mark those rows "Not sent yet" / "sin enviar", and retry after 2, 5, 10 and 30 seconds, then every 30 seconds while visible, and at once on `online`, focus and `visibilitychange`. After 15 seconds unsent, a banner says how many changes are waiting.
5. **An unsent add** shows as a row with no box until it is sent (it has no id yet).
6. **When the reads fail** (on reload, or at any time) but a list is saved, the page draws the saved list under "No connection: this is the list from 3:42pm" (with the day when it isn't today; the time is the database's `as_of`, never the phone's clock), and the queue is sent first when an answer comes. On the Food page this happens whatever the dinner read does; on My day, also when `get_person_day` itself gets no answer (§7.2).
7. **Changes older than 24 hours** are dropped, with one toast.

Why sending twice is safe: every change carries its own key, and the database does it once per key (`private.list_done`): a second try, whose first answer was lost or which a reloaded page sends again, gets the first answer and changes nothing, so a resent add never brings back a line someone bought in between, and isn't counted. Without a key, an add merges by name and a resend that changes nothing isn't counted. A buy only buys the line the phone saw open, and an Undo only reopens the purchase the phone saw. A lost reply costs nothing, a change that waited never undoes a newer one, and a change whose answer came after its page closed isn't sent again.

What it doesn't do, honestly:
- An iPhone sends nothing while the page is closed (Safari has no Background Sync), so changes wait until it is opened again, which the banner says. A home-screen icon and Safari keep separate storage.
- With no network at all, neither page can load unless the browser still has it; a service worker for `day.html` is in Later. With weak signal (the page loads, the database calls fail) My day still shows the list saved at home (§7.2).

## 11. Hearth's own meal planning and lists: the honest overlap

- **What Hearth has** (with its paid Family Membership): a **Meals** tab (meals by date and type, per family member, added from the Hearth app, shown on the display), a Recipe Vault in the app, and a **Groceries** list that can be shown on the display.
- **What Home Hub can do with them: nothing.** Hearth has no API and no import or export; its only outside connection is calendar sync. Home Hub can't fill the Meals tab, read it, or touch Groceries. (The research read Hearth's pages through search extracts only; check during the v1.1 Hearth sitting.)
- **Using both means two plans.** Every dinner shows twice on the wall (Meals tab and Family column), the two drift, and two grocery lists mean someone shops from the wrong one.
- **Why Home Hub's by default** (decision 1): Taylor shops with her phone, in Spanish, with no Hearth app; dinner reaches Today, My day, the email and the wall; the lock keeps private items off shared screens; and it doesn't depend on Hearth's membership.
- **What the family gives up:** the Meals tab's look on the display, Hearth's AI helper and photo import, and the list on the wall.
- **If the family prefers Hearth's meal planner:** turn off "Dinners on the kitchen wall" and don't plan dinners in the hub; the list still works on its own. Then the email, Today and My day have no dinner line. If seeing the list on the wall matters more than Taylor's phone, the honest answer is Hearth's Groceries instead of ours, not both.
- **What the family does** with the defaults: plan dinners and keep the list in Home Hub, and leave Hearth's Meals and Groceries empty. Anything already in Hearth stays there; there is no way to move it.
- **Nothing in Home Hub hides Hearth's tabs.** They stay on the display and in the Hearth app, and anything typed there reaches neither Taylor, the email nor the hub. So at the v1.1 Hearth sitting (§15 step 5, §16.2) we look for a Hearth setting that hides Meals and Groceries on the display and in the app, and use it if there is one. If there isn't, the README says it plainly: "Anything added to Hearth's Meals or Groceries won't reach Taylor, the email or the hub. Add it on the Food page."

## 12. Security and privacy

1. **The first free text written without a sign-in.** `add_to_list_by_token` is the first anonymous function that stores typed text. It is contained:
   - only an active helper's valid token, and only while `helper_list = 'add'`;
   - anything over 200 characters refused before any work is done on it; then cleaned (control, bidi and zero-width characters out, one line) and cut to 60 characters, the note to 80;
   - **counted per call** in `my_day_use`: at most 30 changes to the list and 100 ticks or Undos a day from My day, whatever they change (a note appended, a line brought back, a tick undone); her parallel calls wait for each other on the counter's row, so the caps are exact; adds from My day stop at 150 things to buy, so the family can always add up to 200;
   - labelled: her adds show her dot on the Food page, and `updated_by` says "<name> (My day)";
   - shown only on the Food page and her own screen, always as escaped text, never as a link;
   - never in an email, a text, Google or the wall.
2. **A leaked My day link** (WhatsApp groups) could read the unlocked list and tick or add within those caps. Every tick of the day is under **Just bought** on the Food page with the ticker's dot, one tap back each; junk is one **Forget** each; **New link** on the Family page (`rotate_day_token(p_person)`) stops the old link at once, and the next answer to the old link's phone is null, which clears what it saved. **Locked lines are never read, matched, changed or named through a token:** a link's add only ever looks at unlocked lines (§4.1), so typing a locked line's name makes a new visible line and tells nothing about the locked one; a link's tick or Undo of a locked id answers exactly as an unknown id (`false`).
3. **What My day never shows:** locked lines; anyone's name except the first name of the person on a meal; email addresses or phone numbers; the title of a locked meal she isn't on; notes and links of meals she isn't on; anything bought before today except names in `suggest`, which is sent only when she can add, and covers 30 days.
4. **The wall is public in the house** (v1.1 §7): only "Dinner: <title> (<name>)" and "<name> cooks", or "Dinner: Private item" for a locked dinner. Lock a dinner whose title visitors shouldn't read (a surprise).
5. **Recipe links** are `https://` and printable ASCII only (a check constraint, so no spaces, quotes, angle brackets, control, bidi or zero-width characters can hide the real address), shown as links only on the Food page and the cook's My day, with the host name as the text and `rel="noopener noreferrer"`.
6. **Grants:** anon has no privilege on the three tables; signed-in non-members see no rows; `shopping_items` can't be inserted directly, `bought_*` and `added_*` can't be written directly, and `my_day_use` can't be read or written by anyone but its definer function; the private writers aren't callable by members. The pinned lists in `access.test.sql` change on purpose, and the PR says why.
7. **On the phone:** the Food page's saved list (locked lines included) belongs to the person who signed in and is removed at sign-out; My day's is kept under a hash of the link, not the link, and removed when the link stops working (§8).
8. **The lock is not a secret from grown-ups.** Every grown-up who signs in sees locked lines and can unlock them; the README and the Edit dialog say so. Gifts for someone who signs in don't belong on this list.
9. **Accepted risk:** any grown-up can change any dinner, forget any item or put back anything. They are the grown-ups of the house, and `updated_by` records who.
10. Nothing new in secrets, Google scopes or `hub-jobs`. No recipe page is ever fetched by the server.

## 16. Later, checks during the build, and found while reading

### 16.1 Later (each is additive)

- **Lunch and breakfast:** a page change only; the table has `slot`.
- **Meals on the Calendar page;** copy last week; theme nights; "haven't had in a while" suggestions; a Sunday "plan the week?" line in the parents' email.
- **The list on the wall** as one all-day line ("Groceries (12): Milk, Eggs…"), if the family wants it.
- **A bigger bilingual word list,** and matching multi-word names across languages ("ground beef" / "carne molida"); v1.2 matches whole names from about 150 pairs.
- **Taylor corrects an aisle** from My day.
- **A service worker** for `day.html`, so the list opens with no network at all; **Realtime** for signed-in phones (needs a publication in the stand-ins and websocket fakes).
- **The signed-in hub starting with no signal:** the core keeps the last good `whoami`, schema version and people, and doesn't fall back to the v1 pages when `hub_schema_version()` gets no answer (§8, §16.3). A core change for every page, so not in v1.2.
- **"Undo what Taylor's link did today"** for parents, in one tap: reopen her ticks of the day and forget the lines she made that nobody touched since. It needs a `created_at` / `created_by` on `shopping_items` to tell a new line from one brought back. Until then, Just bought shows every tick of the day, the caps bound the rest, and New link stops it.

- **Kid requests** ("Jordan asked for…"), approved by a parent.
- **Long-press to tick** against pocket taps; screen wake lock while the list is open.

**Not at all:** nutrition, calories and diets; a recipe manager (steps, photos, scaling); pantry stock and expiry dates; quantities merged with unit maths; AI meal plans and photo import; delivery links; Alexa and other voice assistants (Amazon closed its list API in 2024); prices and budgets; a notification per added item; barcode scanning; syncing with Hearth's Meals or Groceries, AnyList or OurGroceries (none has a public API).

## 17. Where this design came from

Three drafts were written independently and scored (simplicity, weekly use, helper safety, fit, buildability). This design starts from the minimal one and grafts:
- **From the minimal draft:** the scope (one list, one dinner line); one row per name, kept forever, as history, suggestions and duplicate guard; tap = bought with Undo and a list of bought things as a free staples list ("Buy again" since the review); polling; wrapping `digest_for` and `calendar_desired` instead of copying them; the honest Hearth section.
- **From the integration-first draft:** the per-item lock instead of a second list; the `slot` column; the two `household` switches; the `calendar_plan` branch that keeps past dinners, and check 5c; amounts parsed into a note; aisles stored on the item's own row; dinner alone emailing only the cook; dinner kept out of `person_items`.
- **From the helper-first draft:** the optional cook with a note and link shown only to the cook; Spanish aisle headings; the shared outbox with "not sent yet" marks; a separate read function for polling; "Add a prep reminder…" opening the task form; Send Taylor the list; big whole-row taps on My day.
- **Turned down for v1.2:** recipes with ingredient lists, staples schedules, several lists, a batch change protocol, a per-minute limit table (the review added a per-day counter instead, §3.2), prep tasks linked to meals (and the `roll_missed_tasks` change they need), meals on the Calendar page, and meals as agenda rows (ten replaced functions). Each adds build and test work, or something to keep up, for little weekly gain; most are in §16.1.
